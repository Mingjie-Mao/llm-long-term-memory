# Does the raw fallback need embeddings? — evidence recall diagnostic

**Measured 2026-08-15 against `stores/two-stage-hydrated.db`. No API calls.**

The fallback searches raw turns with BM25. Adding dense retrieval would mean
embedding every turn, a second index, more disk, more ingest cost and a model in the
serving path — so the question is whether BM25 actually has a gap, and the way to
answer it is evidence recall, not end-to-end accuracy. Accuracy folds retrieval,
answering and judging into one number and cannot say which moved.

Direct question: **when a question needs a raw turn, does that turn come back?**

Gold turns come from LongMemEval's own `has_answer` flags, so nothing here is
hand-labelled. 31 questions in the ingested namespaces carry them.

## Headline table

| retriever | query type | n | R@1 | R@3 | R@5 | MRR | median latency |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| bm25 | keyword | 31 | 93.5% | 96.8% | 96.8% | 0.946 | 7.8ms |
| bm25 | natural | 31 | 54.8% | 74.2% | 74.2% | 0.640 | 16.3ms |
| bm25 | disjoint | 29 | 6.9% | 6.9% | 6.9% | 0.069 | 3.4ms |

* `keyword` — salient terms lifted from the gold turn. BM25's best case, an upper
  bound rather than a realistic query.
* `natural` — LongMemEval's own question, as a user would ask it.
* `disjoint` — the question with every content word it shares with the gold turn
  mechanically removed.

## The `disjoint` row does not mean what it looks like

**6.9% is an artifact of how those queries were built, and must not be cited as
evidence for embeddings.** A manual spot check — the step that was supposed to
validate the construction — showed the deletion removes the *meaning* along with the
overlapping words:

```
natural : How long have I been collecting vintage cameras?
disjoint: "long"

natural : What health issue did I initially think was just a cold?
disjoint: "health issue think"

natural : How many new postcards have I added to my collection...?
disjoint: (empty)
```

`"long"` is not the same question asked in different words; it is not a question.
No retriever recovers the gold turn from it, dense included. The row measures
degraded input, not lexical brittleness.

## Hand-written paraphrases: BM25 holds up

The Mayo Clinic case (`41275add`), five phrasings of one question, gold turn
`answer_sharegpt_81riySf_0:1`:

| phrasing | rank |
|---|---:|
| "mayo clinic youtube video" | **1** |
| "the posture video you recommended" | **1** |
| "what was that desk ergonomics resource you gave me" | **1** |
| "the video I could share with my employees about sitting properly" | **1** |
| "what did you suggest when we discussed workplace back pain" | **1** |

Rank 1 on all five, including phrasings with no "Mayo", no "Clinic", no "YouTube".
The reason is that genuine paraphrases still share *topic* words — posture,
ergonomics, sitting, back pain all appear in the source turn. What they drop is the
proper noun, and BM25 never needed it.

## The 26% that `natural` misses is not a retrieval problem

Eight of 31 natural queries miss at depth 5. Every one belongs to a category where
no single turn is the answer:

| category | n | example | why retrieval cannot fix it |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `single-session-preference` | 3 | "trouble with my battery life, any tips?" | the gold is a rubric; the question contains nothing to match |
| `temporal-reasoning` | 2 | "how many weeks ago did I receive the chandelier?" | the answer is computed from dates, not stated in a turn |
| `multi-session` | 3 | "how many museums did I visit in February?" | the answer aggregates across turns |

Dense retrieval does not help any of these. "How many museums did I visit in
February" has no single gold turn to rank first — the count exists only after
aggregating several. Swapping the retriever changes which wrong turn comes back.

## Decision

**Do not add embeddings to the raw fallback.** The evidence for a lexical gap
dissolved on inspection: the one row that showed a gap was a construction artifact,
hand-written paraphrases retrieve at rank 1, and the genuine misses are task-shaped
rather than vocabulary-shaped.

This is not a claim that BM25 is sufficient in general. It is a claim about this
store and this question distribution, with two conditions that would reopen it:

1. **A real paraphrase set.** These five were hand-written for one case. A proper
   test needs paraphrases generated by a model over 30–50 cases, which costs quota;
   worth doing if fallback usage grows.
2. **A different corpus.** LongMemEval sessions are topically dense, so a
   paraphrase almost always reuses a topic word. A store of terser turns would give
   lexical matching less to hold onto.

Reproduce with `evaluation/raw_recall.py`; it makes no API calls.
